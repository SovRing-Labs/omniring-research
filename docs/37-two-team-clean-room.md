## The author's idea, verbatim
On 10-07: *"so we can't have a group of 'doctors'/coder look at a codebase, output the equations/symbols for the functions, and give those to
the implementor doctor? with chrome it was looking at the blackbox with the same process … it's not copying code."* And the larger picture:
*"so essentially we can have an entire cleanroom setup fully made up of 'doctor cores' where a system scrapes or downloads specs or code,
indexes them, 1-3 doctors interpret into equations, another 2-3 doctors builds them into our own database, and another 1-3 doctors are just
building whatever app is on the que, with no giant llm overhead, pure determenistic logic and reason under 100mb per head??"*

### The two-team clean room: readers, a wall, an implementer
- **Design (field term: two-team clean-room design with a barrier, as in the PC BIOS re-implementations of the early 1980s; the company names and dates are to be checked against a published history):** READERS see source that the code index admitted (our own, or a permissive-licence crate), or only a
  black box's behaviour, and write, per function, a name-free SPEC; the WALL passes only function; one IMPLEMENTER sees the spec and our own library and never the source. The "never read" guarantee holds on the implementer side and in the wall, not for the readers.
  No large model is in the loop: lowering, the wall and the implementer are deterministic programs with exact verifiers (a compiler, examples, equation equivalence).
- **Spec (JSON per function; `core --abc spec`):** typed arguments (int, f32, int list, f32 list), the equation over the argument slots (the A1 term), observed examples,
  held-out rows, validate rows, effects, provenance (source hash + licence). No names, lines, spans or comments. (harness: harness/part7/ch4-engine/src/doctor_spec.rs; result: docs/appendix/part7/ch4-cleanroom/results/s1-s3/specs.layout.jsonl (row layout))
- **Wall (`core --abc wall`):** (1) a strict schema whitelist and an equation grammar (exactly one well-formed term; heads only in head position; nothing after the
  root); (2) a leak scan of every string value against the source's identifiers (≥ 3 chars, non-keyword), comment words and string literals, whole-word, with the wall's
  own vocabulary (keys, alphabet operators, reader id, licence names, type names) exempt because the schema pins it. A seeded-leak harness injects 8 kinds of leak per spec
  (a name into the need, a name as a new key, a comment word, a literal in the equation, a name as an equation argument, a literal in an example, a name in a provenance key, code text in the equation) and the wall must refuse every one. (harness: harness/part7/ch4-engine/src/doctor_spec.rs `wall`; result: docs/appendix/part7/ch4-cleanroom/results/s1-s3/wall.layout.json)
- **Implementer (`core --abc need … --emit | --build --ff [--oracle-map]`):** route 0 prints the spec equation as typed Rust (wrapping semantics; `while` → a loop over
  a state tuple; lists, folds, tuples, f32), verified on the spec rows and 2,000 oracle rows (rows read from a data file: 2,032 rows checked per spec); route 1 synthesises (frame-first, CEGIS)
  with a library that EXCLUDES the source file; an equation gate (`spec::equiv`) parses the spec equation back into the e-graph and runs it as an oracle on 10,000 inputs
  (signed edges, i32 bounds, three random scales, lists of 0..8 elements), feeding any disagreement back as a counterexample. (harness: harness/part7/ch4-engine/src/doctor_spec.rs, harness/part7/ch4-engine/src/doctor_abc.rs; result: docs/appendix/part7/ch4-cleanroom/results/s1-s3/impl-emit.layout.jsonl)

- FIELD PREDICTS: with a weak test set, repairs that pass the tests are often wrong (Smith et al. 2015) — the examples-only gate should pass overfits, and a stronger oracle (the spec equation as an oracle on
  10,000 inputs, CEGIS feedback) should remove them; counterexample-guided repair converges when the counterexamples cover the failing region (Solar-Lezama et al. 2006). Oracle-guided component-based synthesis
  (Jha et al. 2010) succeeds on loop-free compositions from input–output queries and struggles with loops and recursion — `div_ceil` (loop-free) was rebuilt from behaviour, the Euclid loop and the roots were not.
  Floating-point inner products obey |fl(x·y) − x·y| ≤ γₙ·Σ|xᵢyᵢ| (Higham, Accuracy and Stability of Numerical Algorithms, §3.1), so parity must use a summation bound, and plain relative error can be
  large under cancellation (as measured: 2 + 88 rows flagged at 10⁻⁴). RMSNorm (Zhang & Sennrich 2019) and linear attention with feature map elu + 1 (Katharopoulos et al. 2020) are the published mathematics the
  equations were authored from. Two-team clean-room design predicts that leakage through identifiers and comments is the failure to guard — hence the leak scan and the seeded harness.

### The model doctor (the same census → blame → cluster, applied to a training run)
- **Description (`harness/part7/ch4-model-doctor/model_doctor.py`):** for each doing / retry attempt in a run's journal, ONE cause, first match wins: solved on its own, solved with a harness consult at the answer, runaway (loop guard
